# Prototyping parity

> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-11 · **Last updated:** 2026-10-15 · **Change:** minor (presenter controls: one shortcut table with a `?` list, "3 / 12" with previous and next, hide UI, a background colour, a screens panel with thumbnails and a window of its own for the presentation; video in prototypes: video fills that play in the presenter, the "On media hit" and "On media end" triggers and the play/pause, mute, skip and skip-to actions, with the video formats that really play; open link in the same tab: an "Open in a new tab" setting on the action, REST `openInNewTab`, same-tab navigation in the web version; which addresses a link may open; core editor leftovers before them: delays on mouse triggers and manual overlay offsets; controller buttons on key/gamepad triggers, widgets that react in prototypes, `proto.evaluate` and the mode lookup rules pinned) · **Target:** Figma Design (desktop app 126.10.7)

**Breadth ~94%, ready ~74% (estimated).** Every Figma trigger, action, transition and easing exists;
key/gamepad triggers take controller buttons, read from controllers on Windows and in the browser.
Media plays as far as the decoders go: Motion-JPEG video and animated GIF, WebP and PNG play, and the
H.264, HEVC, VP9 and AV1 video most files hold is refused by name
([Supported video](#supported-video)), so ready stays where it was. Every rule of the public
documentation on triggers, actions, overlays, animations, easing, scrolling and interactive
components is a test case with what it implies (`crates/engine/src/proto_behaviour_tests.rs`, 7
tests on the model, and `crates/ui-egui/src/present_behaviour_tests.rs`, 12 tests driving the
player, 21 gestures among them; the tail: `crates/engine/src/proto_tail_tests.rs`, 4 tests, and
`crates/ui-egui/src/present_tail_tests.rs`, 5 tests), and all pass. Not yet checked against
larger real prototypes. Remaining: 2–5 h (controllers off Windows, real prototype QA, the presenter's own window seen on real desktops).

## Rules from the documentation

Status: **pass** (a case in the suite holds), **fixed** (the suite found a divergence, now fixed),
**added** (the setting was missing and now exists), **silent** (the documentation doesn't say; the
case pins the current behaviour).

## Presenter controls

Viewing aids of the full-window presenter and the preview pane: none of them is saved in the file.
The code is in `crates/ui-egui/src/present_chrome.rs` (the module `present::ux`; the side panel and the
separate window have files of their own), the tests in `present_chrome_tests.rs`. Options that are
the viewer's (`PresentView`) are kept between presentations, shown by `ui.inspect` (`presentOptions`,
and `presenter` while presenting) and set through `ui.set`.

| Control | Behaviour | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shortcut list | The `?` button in the bar, the `?` key and Options ▸ Keyboard shortcuts open a window listing every key of the presenter under its section, each key as a keycap; the keys still work while it shows; Escape (once), `?` or its close button close it | **one table** (`SHORTCUTS`) holds the acts and their keys: the key handling (`act_for`), the hints in the Options menu (`spec`) and the list (`help_window`) all read it, so they can't disagree. `every_listed_key_does_what_the_list_says` presses every listed key and checks its effect (a `match` without a catch-all on the act: a new act needs a case), and the list's test checks that every row and key is on screen. A key the prototype uses itself (a key trigger) goes to the prototype first, as before; only Shift+C wins over it |
| Screen counter | "3 / 12" beside the screen's name with previous and next buttons (also in the preview pane when it fits: buttons and text, else the text alone, else nothing) | **X** is the screen's place among the page's top-level frames in reading order (rows top to bottom, then left to right; frames inside sections count), **Y** how many there are: exactly the sequence the right and left arrows, Space and N step through (`present::ux::sequence`), whatever flows the page has, since a flow is only a starting point and flows share screens. It follows every way the screen changes (keys, the buttons, a link, Back, restart). A page with one frame has no counter. With an overlay open it keeps the screen under it, and the buttons and keys wait until the overlay closes (as the keys always did). The name gives way to it on a narrow bar. Tests: `the_bar_says_which_screen_of_the_page_this_is_and_steps_with_buttons`, `an_overlay_keeps_the_counter_…`, `a_page_with_one_screen_has_no_counter_…` |
| Hide UI | Options ▸ Hide UI and `Cmd/Ctrl+\` hide the bar and the Inspect panel: the prototype takes the whole window, centred and fitted again to it. The pointer within a few points of the window's top edge slides the bar back in over the prototype (taking the pointer from it) while it stays on the bar or a menu of the bar is open; `Cmd/Ctrl+\` again, or Escape once, bring the UI back for good (a second Escape closes) | a viewing option kept for the next presentation: `presentOptions.hideUi`, `presenter.hideUi`, `presenter.barReveal`, `ui.set {presentHideUi}`. | Background colour | Options ▸ Background (Page colour, Dark, Mid grey, Light, White, Black, each with its swatch, and a hex field) or the B key (round them) pick the colour around the prototype in the full-window presenter | "Page colour" is the default and what it always was: the Background of the page's Prototype settings. A viewing option, **not** saved in the file (the file and its unsaved state stay as they were), kept for the next presentation: `presentOptions.background`, `presenter.background` (`page` or `#RRGGBB`), `ui.set {presentBackground}` (`page`, `dark`, `grey`, `light`, `white`, `black` or a hex colour; the field takes the colour fields' hex forms, transparency ignored). On a device the screen behind the frame keeps the page's colour. Readable on light colours: a pale device body gets a soft shadow so its outline stays, the empty pane's text turns dark; the bar, the Inspect panel and the lists have colours of their own, and the hotspot hints and Inspect outline are blue, which reads on both. The menu stays open while colours are tried |
| Screens panel | The first button of the bar (an icon), the S key and `ui.set {presentSidePanel}` open a panel at the left (232 wide) listing the page's screens, each with a thumbnail, its number and its name, under the flows. ## Supported video

What plays in a prototype, and what is refused, as of 2026-10-15. The pictures come from
`crates/media` (`figcraft-media`), which reads the containers itself from their public
specifications and decodes pictures with the JPEG decoder of the `image` crate. Nothing in the
product links a C library for it.

- **Plays:** Motion-JPEG (every picture a JPEG) in **AVI** (`MJPG`), and in **MOV and MP4**
  (`jpeg`, or MPEG-4 Visual objects of the JPEG type); **animated GIF, WebP and PNG** (APNG), as videos
  when placed with `video: true` (or by the colour picker's Video type), else they stay the pictures
  that always animated by themselves. Seeking is exact (every Motion-JPEG picture stands alone).
- **Refused, by name:** every other video codec: H.264 (AVC), HEVC, VP8, VP9, AV1, ProRes,
  MPEG-4 Visual and the other codecs of AVI and MOV files, DV, Cinepak and so on; WebM and Matroska
  files, by their codec. The message says `This video codec isn't supported yet: H.264 (avc1)`, and
  the file is not kept in the document. Fragmented MP4 (`moof`) is refused too. Most video that
  people have is H.264 or HEVC in MP4/MOV or VP9 in WebM, so most files will be refused until a
  decoder exists; a Motion-JPEG copy plays (for example, with FFmpeg: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -c:v mjpeg
  -q:v 3 -an out.avi`; FFmpeg is a tool here, nothing is linked or shipped).
- **Sound:** not played. Files with sound open (the audio track is ignored; `video.info` and the
  colour picker say `hasAudio`); muting only changes the state that mute and unmute actions toggle.
- **Limits:** 100 MB a file, 36 million pixels (and 16,384 pixels a side) a picture, 500,000
  pictures, 24 hours; animated images at most 500 pictures and 128 MB decoded. Hostile or damaged
  files give an error or the pictures before the damage, never a panic (fuzzed: truncations,
  bit flips and random bytes on every container and the clock).
- **Where it shows:** video fills play in the presenter and the preview pane; the canvas and the
  PNG, JPG, WebP, SVG and PDF exports show the poster picture. `.fig` import skips video fills and
  `.fig` export leaves them out, with a warning. A video travels in the `.figcraft` file like an
  image (and in a live session as part of the document, within its message limit).
- **Cost:** a picture is decoded when it is first needed and a few are kept, so large pictures can
  make playback stutter on a slow machine (not measured on real HD files); a file of up to 100 MB is
  held in memory.
